# Point economy and the level → points mapping

Locked decision 2: the budget is generous — 1.5–2× the meaningful build choices
RAW offers over a 20-level career — and the baseline is **computed from the
extraction**, not guessed. This file records what was computed and the ratio
Task 3 asks to be documented.

Everything here is regenerated by `src/dnd2024/point_economy.py` and written to
`data/output/point_economy.json`.

## What counts as a RAW build choice

For each class, counted straight off `graph.json`:

- every class feature of that class (283 nodes across 12 classes)
- the features of **one** subclass, averaged over that class's four subclasses
  (a RAW character picks one)
- the optional-feature picks the class table grants at level 20 — Warlock's 10
  invocations and Sorcerer's 6 metamagic come from
  `classes_meta.optionalfeature_progression`; Battle Master's 9 maneuvers are a
  subclass table Work Order 1 did not extract and are stated explicitly in
  `SUBCLASS_OPTIONAL_PICKS`
- the weapon masteries the class table grants (Fighter 4, Barbarian/Paladin/
  Ranger/Rogue 3)

ASI and feat slots are **not** added separately: they are already in the class
feature counts as `Ability Score Improvement` nodes (51 of them), and counting
them twice would inflate every martial class.

| Rogue | 42 | Ranger | 32 |

| Warlock | 36 | Druid | 30 |

Mean **33.33**. Artificer is excluded — the extraction has no Artificer
features, so it has no baseline to contribute.

## The budget

```
total points at level 20 = round(33.33 × 1.75) = 58
```

58 points against a 33-choice RAW career: a player can afford roughly 1.75×
what RAW gives them *if they stay home*, since their own zone's connector spine
is free to walk. Cross-zone shopping costs about 6 points per acquisition
(measured — see `validation_report.json`), so a build that ranges widely buys
noticeably less. That is the intended shape: breadth is the thing you pay for.

## The mapping ratio (Task 3)

RAW level prerequisites become **points-spent thresholds**. One convention,
applied everywhere:

> a `character level N` prerequisite becomes
> `threshold_count = cumulative points at the end of level N-1`

so the gate opens exactly when a character who had been levelling normally would
have banked enough points to have earned it. A level 1 prerequisite becomes 0 —
no gate at all.

| RAW level | 2 | 4 | 5 | 9 | 11 | 12 | 15 | 19 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| points spent | 6 | 14 | 17 | 29 | 34 | 36 | 44 | 53 |

Class-level prerequisites (`Warlock level 5`) use the same table. In a
decoupled system there is no class level to read, and the intent of the RAW gate
is "you have invested this much", which is what points spent measures.

Worked examples from the built graph:

- Ability Score Improvement, RAW `level 4` → `THRESHOLD 14`
- Agonizing Blast, RAW `Warlock level 2` → `THRESHOLD 6`
- Devouring Blade, RAW `Warlock level 12` + Thirsting Blade →
  `THRESHOLD 36` **and** `of_thirsting_blade_xphb` owned
- Boon of Spell Recall, RAW `level 19` + spellcasting → `THRESHOLD 53` **and**
  any first-tier slot node owned

## What this does not do

Thresholds are checked against **total points spent**, not points spent in a
zone. The alternative convention Task 3 offered ("own N nodes in this zone")
was rejected because it re-introduces class identity through the back door: it
would mean a character's Warlock invocations gate on how Warlock-ish they are,
which is exactly the thing locked decision 1 removes. Total spend is
class-agnostic and matches "you have been adventuring this long".
